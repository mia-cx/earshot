> [!TLDR] Google ranks pages, not companies, so a strong page on a site that already ranks can reach searchers before your own site can.
> - When other platforms rank for your query, publish a complete answer there and link back to your site.
> - Where Google shows videos, publish a demo of your product doing that exact task.
> - Use Google Trends to answer rising questions before anyone else does.

## Borrow a site Google already trusts

**A new site takes months to rank, while some platforms already do.** Google's [ranking systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) work mostly page by page with some sitewide signals, so a page on a trusted platform starts with that platform's standing.

Search your target query. If LinkedIn articles, Medium posts or forum threads sit on page one, Google is happy to rank that platform for it. Publish a complete answer there and link to your site as the next step.

> [!RISK] Where this becomes spam
> People call this "parasite SEO", a name tied to abuse Google penalises. Google's [site reputation abuse policy](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) targets content placed on a host "mainly because of that host's already-established ranking signals", such as a third-party casino ad page on a medical site. Platforms built for user content, like forums, and signed opinion pieces are named as fine. Publish under your own name, on platforms where anyone can post, and write for that platform's readers.

Keep it simple: put the topic in the headline and the answer up top. Write for that platform's readers, not as a copy of your blog post, and link to one relevant next step, not five.

> [!TRICK] Rank a LinkedIn article
> - Pick queries LinkedIn already ranks for.
> - Put the query early in the headline. The headline sets the article's URL, so check it before you publish.
> - Lead with the answer and an example.
> - Link to the LinkedIn article from your own site.

## Make videos where Google shows videos

**When Google puts videos on a results page, that's an opening for a product demo.** Google's [video guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/video) says it shows "key moments" inside videos and prefers the ones you set yourself through chapters in the YouTube description.

Search your buyer questions and note which ones show video results. If you sell software, check your "best X" query on a phone too: a video row there means it's worth making one.

> [!TRICK] Publish a demo for a video search
> 1. Confirm videos appear for the query.
> 2. Pick one specific product question.
> 3. Record the product completing that exact task. Make the long-form video first; cut short clips from it later.
> 4. Name the task in the video title.
> 5. Add chapters starting at 00:00: YouTube needs [at least three, each 10 seconds or longer](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9884579). Add captions you've corrected by hand.
> 6. Answer related buyer questions in the description.
> 7. Embed the video next to the written answer on your site.

## Answer rising searches early

**When something changes in your field, being early is the advantage.** New questions appear before good answers exist, and Google's [freshness systems](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/appearance/ranking-systems-guide) favour newer pages where people expect them. Explain the news through your own expertise instead of repeating it.

Google Trends tracks [relative interest from a sample](https://support.google.com/trends/answer/4365533) on a 0 to 100 scale, not search volume. Use it to spot a rise, then check volume in a keyword tool.

> [!TRICK] Ride a rising search with Google Trends
> 1. Filter Google Trends by your category and country.
> 2. Check the history to tell seasonal demand from a real spike.
> 3. Read the original announcement, not the coverage of it.
> 4. Explain what changed and what it means for your buyers.
> 5. Send your finding to reporters who cover the topic.

For reshaping one piece of research into each platform's own format, see *Turn one conversation into ten posts*.

> [!FORM] Your borrowed platform
> 1. What are your three most important buyer questions?
> 2. Which third-party platforms (LinkedIn, YouTube, Reddit, Medium) show up on page one for them?
> 3. Which one platform will you publish on?
> 4. The headline you'd publish there, with the query early.

> [!DO] Find one platform that ranks for you
> Search your three most important buyer questions in a private window. Fill in your borrowed platform above, then download it.

> [!CHECK] Before you write about a rising search in Google Trends, what do you check?
> Its history, to tell seasonal demand from a real spike. Then read the original announcement, not the coverage of it.

> [!CHECK] Google shows videos on the results page for one of your buyer questions. What do you make?
> A video of your product completing that exact task, with the task named in the title. Embed it next to the written answer on your site.

> [!CHECK] When your own site is new, why publish answers on platforms like LinkedIn or Medium?
> A new site takes months to earn rankings, and those platforms already rank for your query. Your answer there links back to your site as the next step.
