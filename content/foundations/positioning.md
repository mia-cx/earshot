> [!TLDR] Positioning is the context that makes your value obvious to the buyers who care most.
> - Start from what buyers would do without you, including nothing.
> - Answer the positioning questions before you post or launch anything. A blank means you don't understand the product yet.
> - Name what you're against, then write your messaging around it.
> - Picture one reader's situation in detail before you write a word.

## Write your positioning down first

Positioning lives in your buyer's mind, an idea Al Ries and Jack Trout [made famous in 1972](https://ries.com/about/history-of-positioning). April Dunford defines it as how your product leads at something a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about ([quickstart guide](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning)).

Dunford's method starts with **competitive alternatives**: what buyers would do if you didn't exist. A difference only counts against what they actually compare you with.

Write yours in one document before you market anything. One marketer (@kunalvg) says that if you can't answer a question below, **you don't understand the product well enough yet**.

> [!FORM] Your positioning doc
> 1. What do we do? What problem do we solve? Give one short, precise answer.
> 2. Which market category do we compete in?
> 3. Why is our approach the best? What sets us apart?
> 4. What are our unique attributes or features, and their key benefits? Think about approach, process or intellectual property.
> 5. Who would be most interested? What do the most excited users have in common, and where can we find them?
> 6. If not us, what would users choose instead? Include doing nothing.
> 7. What are we against? Name a status quo, a bad habit or an incumbent way of doing things.

Answer questions 1 to 6 in Dunford's order. Each answer builds on the one before:

1. Question 6: the alternatives buyers really consider.
2. Questions 3 and 4: what you have that they don't.
3. Question 1: what that does for buyers.
4. Question 5: who cares most.
5. Question 2: the category that makes it obvious.

Question 7 isn't part of Dunford's method. It's this course's messaging exercise, covered in *Name the enemy* below.

For a deeper version, work through Dunford's [positioning exercise](https://hackernoon.com/obviously-awesome-a-product-positioning-exercise-604e8ced841e).

## What weak positioning breaks

In @kunalvg's experience, unclear positioning breaks two things at once. Your product drifts because nobody is obsessed with a specific user. Your go-to-market plan stays thin: no clear ideal customer, no chosen channels, no plan to grow them.

Both trace back to the same blank page. Fix the page first.

## Name the enemy

Win the conviction battle before the distribution battle. Decide who or what your product stands against, write your messaging around it, and only then plan where to post. Dunford agrees: buyers often choose to do nothing, so argue against the status quo.

- If you don't strongly believe in the product, fix that first.
- Pick a clear opposing force: a status quo, a bad habit, an incumbent way of doing things.
- Be opinionated. @kunalvg argues middle-of-the-road messaging no longer stands out.

> [!RISK] Most buyers won't notice your difference
> Across 17 markets, only 17% of a brand's own buyers on average called it different or unique ([Romaniuk, Sharp and Ehrenberg, 2007](https://byronsharp.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/04/different1.pdf)). Buyers also have to recognise you, so look and sound the same everywhere you show up.

## Picture one reader

Your positioning names a group. Your writing needs a person.

Clayton Christensen's [jobs to be done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) theory says people "hire" a product to make progress in a specific situation. That situation explains their choice better than demographics. Picture one reader in that situation before you write, and add more detail the closer the piece is to a sale.

> [!FORM] Your reader
> - What are they struggling with right now?
> - Where do they read? Are they even on X?
> - What do they want out of this?
> - What are they feeling right now?

*Write for one reader and one action* picks this up when you start writing posts.

> [!DO] Write your positioning doc
> Fill in the positioning doc above, one or two sentences per question, starting with question 6. Download it when you're done. Leave a blank where you're unsure. Every blank is your research list for *Research a market in an afternoon*.

> [!CHECK] In April Dunford's positioning method, where do you start, and why?
> With competitive alternatives: what buyers would do if you didn't exist, including nothing. A difference only counts against what buyers really compare you with.

> [!CHECK] Your messaging tries to appeal to everyone and nobody remembers it. What do you add?
> Name what you're against: a status quo, a bad habit or an incumbent way of doing things. Then write your messaging around that opposing force.

> [!CHECK] Why does weak positioning hurt the product as well as the marketing?
> In one marketer's experience, the product drifts because nobody is obsessed with a specific user. The go-to-market plan also ends up with no clear ideal customer or chosen channels.
