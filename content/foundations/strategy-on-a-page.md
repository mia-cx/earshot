> [!TLDR] A strategy names the obstacle, picks an approach and lists actions that fit it. A goal alone isn't one.
> - Keep the whole strategy on one page with four sections.
> - Plan every task in one order: goal, audience, creative, channel.
> - Plan three months at a time, and pair each outcome goal with inputs you control.
> - Run the work from a board, a calendar and a weekly review of the numbers.

## Keep the strategy to one page

Strategy professor Richard Rumelt names mistaking goals for strategy as a hallmark of [bad strategy](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-perils-of-bad-strategy). A good strategy has three parts: a **diagnosis** of the main obstacle, a **guiding policy** for getting past it, and **coherent actions** that support each other. In Rumelt's words, without a named obstacle you have "a stretch goal or a budget or a list of things you wish would happen".

A strategy you can't read in one sitting won't get used, so keep it to one page with four sections. Your positioning doc from *Say what you are, for whom, against what* feeds the first two. Write the growth strategy as Rumelt's first two parts: the obstacle, then your approach to it. The initiatives are the coherent actions.

## Decide in four steps, in order

Run every marketing task through the GACC brief, which Emily Kramer developed while leading marketing at Asana ([MKT1](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/the-gacc-marketing-brief-the-best)): **goal, then audience, then creative, then channel**. The order matters. The channel comes last because you can't choose where to post until you know what the post is for and who should see it.

> [!TRICK] Write a GACC brief for every task
> - One brief per task, four headings: Goal, Audience, Creative, Channel.
> - Fill them in that order. Don't pick a channel until the first three are done.
> - In a new role, use it on day 2 to set three messaging pillars: "What we build", "Why now", "Who it's for".
> - Template and explanation from the MKT1 newsletter: https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/the-gacc-marketing-brief-the-best

## Plan three months at a time

Agree on objectives with anyone who has a stake, then write a three-month plan with goals you can actually hit. Split it into weekly actions.

Pair every outcome goal (a macro KPI) with input goals you control (micro KPIs). You can't force the outcome, but you can force the inputs. Amazon runs on the same idea: Jeff Bezos wrote that focusing on "the controllable inputs to our business" is the best way to grow its financial results over time ([2009 letter](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312510082914/dex991.htm)).

| Type | Example |
| --- | --- |
| Macro KPI (outcome) | Improve bounce rate by 10% over 3 months |
| Micro KPI (input) | Publish 3 articles a month, each reaching a set number of visits |

In Google Analytics 4, bounce rate is the share of sessions that weren't engaged: 10 seconds or less, no key event, and only one page or screen ([Google](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12195621?hl=en)).

Report weekly and monthly from Google Analytics and Search Console. If goal-setting is new to you, HubSpot's [SMART goal examples](https://blog.hubspot.com/marketing/smart-goal-examples) are a good start. This approach comes from one marketer's four years building a marketing function through to the company's acquisition.

> [!FORM] Your one-page strategy
> 1. Brand promise: what do we promise every customer?
> 2. Target audience: who exactly are we for?
> 3. Growth strategy: how will we grow over the next three months?
> 4. Marketing initiatives: which projects will we run to get there?
> 5. Macro KPI: which outcome do we want in three months?
> 6. Micro KPI: which input that we control drives it?

## Run the week from a board and a calendar

The same marketer runs execution with three tools:

- a kanban board (Notion) for the big picture
- a calendar for daily tasks
- a weekly review of the data, where you double down on what worked

> [!CHECK] Your plan says "grow revenue 20% this year". Why isn't that a strategy yet?
