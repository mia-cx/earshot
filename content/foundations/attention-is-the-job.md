> [!TLDR] Marketing is getting the right people to notice a product worth noticing.
> - Brands grow mainly by winning more buyers, and buyers pick what comes to mind.
> - Most of marketing happens inside the product. When marketing isn't working, go one circle in.
> - A community grows around a good product. It can't replace one.
> - Pick the right strategy before you work hard on execution.

## Attention caps what you can earn

Whatever you build, revenue stops at the number of the right people who know it exists and remember it when they need it. That makes **the ability to capture attention** worth more than another round of product polish.

Byron Sharp's research at the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute found that brands grow mainly by winning more buyers. Winning them takes [mental availability](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-do-you-measure-how-brands-grow): people think of your brand easily when they buy, and recognise it fast.

Attention also has to last. Build a name people keep, not only this week's clicks. Les Binet and Peter Field studied IPA effectiveness cases and found the best results came from about 60% long-term brand building and 40% short-term sales activation ([IPA](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it)).

Dan Koe argues that attention now sits in social feeds, not on TV or radio. Startups call this "founder-led marketing": the person who built the thing talks about it in public. Content here means good content, not a lot of it.

Koe points to two forecasts. Goldman Sachs Research [estimated in 2023](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027) that the creator economy's total addressable market could grow from $250B to $480B by 2027. WPP Media [forecast in June 2025](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2025/jun/10/social-media-creators-to-overtake-traditional-media-in-ad-revenue-this-year) that creators would pass traditional media in ad revenue that year. Both are projections, not measured results.

## Marketing starts at the product

When your marketing isn't working, go one circle in.

That's Seth Godin's rule from [The circles of marketing](https://seths.blog/2012/07/the-circles-of-marketing/). The product sits in the centre. Around it sit usability, support and atmosphere. Next are your story, price, community and the tribe it's for. Only the outermost ring holds ads, spin, buzz, hype, clicks and "getting the word out", the part most people call marketing.

Spend your effort from the centre outwards:

1. Product
2. Usability and support
3. Story, price, community, tribe
4. Ads, hype, clicks

If a user can't figure out your product, no ad fixes that.

## Community follows a good product

A great community forms around a great product. Build one before you have something worth gathering around and it won't last. That's one marketer's observation (@kunalvg) from watching many crypto projects.

So marketing's job is simpler than it looks: make sure everyone who should know how good the product is, knows.

## Get the strategy right, then execute

In @kunalvg's view, good execution of the right strategy beats great execution of a mediocre one, and most teams run a mediocre strategy. You still need both.
