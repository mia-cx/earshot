> [!TLDR]
> - Learn skills where AI speeds up someone who knows a field. Skills AI can do alone are losing value.
> - Run any skill through three questions: do people pay for it, does AI speed you up rather than replace you, does it need field knowledge?
> - Be broad across channels and deep in the field you sell into.
> - Practise the skills you need to check AI's work on purpose, because they fade first.

## Learn skills AI amplifies

The skills that pay are the ones where AI speeds up someone who already knows a field. Skills AI can do without you are losing value.

[PwC's 2026 analysis of over a billion job ads](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-reshapes-global-labour-market-into-two-distinct-paths-rewarding-human-skills-pwc-2026-global-ai-jobs-barometer-302798987.html) shows that split. Where AI frees experts to spend more time on judgement, job openings grew twice as fast and pay rose 42% faster than where AI lets non-experts do the work.

Run any skill you're considering through **three questions**:

> [!FORM] Your skill test
> 1. Which three skills are you considering?
> 2. Do people already pay for this result today? Answer yes or no for each skill.
> 3. Does AI make you faster at it, instead of doing it without you? Yes or no for each skill.
> 4. Does it need knowledge of a field, like sales, health or law? Yes or no for each skill.
> 5. Which skill got three yeses?

Three yeses means a skill is worth learning. @notkvtwts cites four studies behind that split. They hold up, and one gap has grown since:

- [PwC, 2026](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-reshapes-global-labour-market-into-two-distinct-paths-rewarding-human-skills-pwc-2026-global-ai-jobs-barometer-302798987.html): jobs asking for AI skills advertise 62% more pay and grew 69%, against 9% for all jobs. That's advertised pay, not what people earn.
- [MIT, 2023](https://news.mit.edu/2023/study-finds-chatgpt-boosts-worker-productivity-writing-0714): professionals did writing tasks 40% faster and 18% better with ChatGPT. [The working paper](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1_0.pdf) shows their time shifting from rough drafting to ideas and editing.
- [Stanford, 2026](https://digitaleconomy.stanford.edu/publications/canaries-in-the-coal-mine/): employment of workers aged 22 to 25 in the jobs most exposed to AI is 19% below where it would be had it grown as fast as in less exposed jobs. That's a growth comparison, not a count of jobs AI replaced. The post quotes 13%, an estimate from a different model in the first 2025 version. On the measure the paper uses now, the gap grew from 15% to 19% between July 2025 and June 2026. Separately, the decline sits in jobs where AI replaces tasks; where AI mostly assists people, employment held up.
- [Lightcast, 2025](https://lightcast.io/resources/blog/beyond-the-buzz-press-release-2025-07-23): 51% of job ads asking for AI skills are outside IT and computer science.

## Cover every channel at a working level

On a small team with a tight budget, you are the whole marketing team. Kunal Gandhi, who built marketing at a data and AI startup through to its acquisition, lists six basics: copywriting, SEO, performance marketing, community, content and go-to-market.

You don't need mastery in each, only enough to run it yourself. This course covers all six at that level, so revisit the modules you skimmed.

## Know your field well enough to talk shop

Breadth gets the work done; **depth in your field** makes the strategy land. Gandhi advises learning the technology well enough not to sound lost with builders, without needing to code. He credits months of reading and talking to experts with reshaping how those strategies got designed.

- Spend months reading and talking to experts in the field.
- Use the product yourself so you feel every friction point.

Gandhi also quotes a Uniswap marketer on where the market is heading: away from agency veterans running old playbooks on products they don't use, and towards people who combine marketing, partner work, real industry knowledge, decent writing and AI tools to fill the gaps. Both write about crypto, though Gandhi says the advice applies outside it.

Breadth and depth don't conflict. Go broad across channels, and go deep in the one field your buyers work in.

## Keep the skills supervision needs

Skills you stop using fade faster than you expect. [A review of 53 studies on skill decay](https://gwern.net/doc/psychology/spaced-repetition/1998-arthur.pdf) found large losses after a year without practice, and thinking skills faded faster than physical ones.

Checking AI's work needs those exact skills. [Lisanne Bainbridge spotted this in 1983](https://ckrybus.com/static/papers/Bainbridge_1983_Automatica.pdf) in factories: automation leaves people to watch machines and take over when they fail, but someone who only watches slowly stops being an expert. [Anthropic found the same pattern among its own staff](https://www.anthropic.com/research/how-ai-is-transforming-work-at-anthropic) and calls it the paradox of supervision: "supervising Claude requires the very coding skills that may atrophy from AI overuse."

When you hit something beyond your understanding, learn it properly instead of pasting in a one-line explanation. For how to tell when you've stopped checking, see *Use AI without surrendering your judgment*.

> [!TRICK] Run a weekly calibration task
> From Addy Osmani's routine:
>
> - **Weekly:** do one real task without an agent, one that would hurt to forget how to do.
> - **Monthly:** write down what you've stopped doing, and decide which of those you're fine letting go.
> - **Anytime:** ask yourself how lost you'd be if AI didn't exist.

> [!DO] Score three skills
> Fill in the skill test above for three skills you're considering, with yes or no for each of the three questions, then download it. The one that gets three yeses is worth learning.

> [!CHECK] What three questions tell you a skill is worth learning now that AI exists?
> Whether people already pay for the result, whether AI makes you faster rather than doing it without you, and whether it needs knowledge of a field.

> [!CHECK] You're a solo marketer whose buyers work in healthcare. How broad or deep should your skills be?
> Broad across channels at a working level, and deep in healthcare.

> [!CHECK] Why practise on purpose the skills you use to check AI's work?
> Skills you stop using fade, and supervising AI needs exactly those skills.
