> [!TLDR]
> - Busy founders and creators pay one AI-equipped person to run their content as a creative director.
> - An agent that already saves you hours every week can be sold to businesses with the same task.
> - Sell outbound only to companies whose offer already sells: high price, proof in a case study.
> - Clients pay for a scarce result. When AI cuts your delivery time, keep the price and take the margin while that result stays hard to get elsewhere.

## Be the creative team a busy founder lacks

Busy founders and creators will pay one AI-skilled person to act as their creative director for a fraction of a team's cost. Dan Koe splits this into two versions: run your own creative team (personal brand, audience, digital products, email list), or run one for clients.

Koe says most founders still post "announcement" content and don't know how influence is built online. Agencies he spoke to say AI moved the human job to **reviewing and rewriting drafts**, with one cutting the day from 12 hours to 4. That direction matches [an MIT experiment with 444 professionals](https://economics.mit.edu/sites/default/files/inline-files/Noy_Zhang_1_0.pdf): with ChatGPT, their time moved from rough drafting to ideas and editing, and the work finished faster.

Koe puts the range at $10k to $75k a month across 1 to 3 clients, "if you're good at this and practice", and warns it isn't a quick win. He also sells Eden, a tool for this exact work, so weigh the numbers accordingly. The skills come from *Write down what makes you you* and *Plan six months of ideas in one sitting*.

## Resell an agent you already run

An agent that replaces a recurring manual task, like a weekly competitor report, saves you hours. Other businesses with the same task will pay for it. Build it for yourself first (see *Run marketing as code*), then sell it.

- Route the results to yourself for approval first.
- Run it for 2 to 3 weeks, fix what breaks, and count the hours saved.
- That count is your pitch. The log from your week audit in *Learn a new skill in 30 days* works the same way.

## Sell outbound only where the offer already sells

Done-for-you LinkedIn outreach works best for B2B companies with high-priced, proven offers. One new customer covers months of your fee, and a case study with a number proves people already buy.

> [!TRICK] Find and pitch outbound clients
> 1. Go to linkedin.com/jobs and search "sdr". Every company that shows up already pays a full salary to get sales calls booked.
> 2. Keep only companies that charge customers $10k or more and whose website has a case study with a number in it.
> 3. Pitch the founder a test: 1,000 DMs to their buyers for $500. The replies from the test are what sell the monthly retainer.
> 4. @DinScales26's math: 1 call per 100 DMs, so 10 calls per 1,000. At $2,500 a month per client, 4 clients is $10k and 8 is $20k.
>
> @DinScales26 sells this service and a sending tool, and the 1% call rate is assumed, not shown. Treat it as a number to test. For writing and testing the DMs themselves, see *Open DMs with a problem buyers already pay to solve*.

## Keep your price when AI cuts the hours

Clients pay for the result, not your time. When AI shrinks the work, keep the price and **take the margin**.

Kyle Hunt's example: an agency audit sold at $5,000 used to take 5 hours by hand. After he fed AI every past audit transcript and the frameworks used, AI took about 30 minutes and human checking took another 30. The price stayed at $5,000. That's one self-reported example from someone who sells agency consulting.

> [!RISK] The margin lasts only while the result is scarce
> If AI lets anyone produce the same result, buyers stop paying for it. [PwC's 2026 analysis of job ads](https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ai-reshapes-global-labour-market-into-two-distinct-paths-rewarding-human-skills-pwc-2026-global-ai-jobs-barometer-302798987.html) found pay rose 42% faster in roles where AI frees experts for judgement than in roles where it lets non-experts do the work. Sell the judgement, not the hours AI now does.

For the checking half, see *Use AI without surrendering your judgment*. See *Read your pricing from your numbers* for when to raise prices.

> [!FORM] Your offer
> 1. Which task do you already do well with AI?
> 2. Who pays for it?
> 3. What result do they get?
> 4. What price would you charge for that result?

> [!DO] Write a three-line offer
> Pick one task you already do well with AI and fill in your offer above: who pays for it, the result they get, and the price you'd charge for that result. Then download it.
