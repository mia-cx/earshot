> [!TLDR]
> - Awe and surprise are among the strongest reasons people share. Your demo creates them.
> - Show the most extreme thing your product can do, in a way nobody can dismiss as fake.
> - In a feed, show the product, make it work without sound, and keep it short.

## The demo earns belief

A good narrative makes someone stop scrolling (see *Give strangers a reason to care*). Your demo has a different job. It has to make the viewer think "this is real". If it looks staged or vague, you lose them.

Jonah Berger and Katherine Milkman's [research on sharing](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353) found people share content that sparks awe or surprise more often. A demo can create both, as long as people believe what they see.

## Push the product to its extreme

**Show the most extreme thing your product can do**, then make the proof impossible to wave away.

Matt Epstein points to Tesla's Roadster launch. The car drives up a curved ramp, hangs upside down, flips through the air, lands on its wheels, then jumps a whole row of Teslas. Jumping one car is neat. Jumping the fleet is what you send to ten friends.

Tesla did it live on a livestream in front of a crowd. [Snopes checked it](https://www.snopes.com/fact-check/tesla-flying-roadster-car-real/): Tesla streamed the event on 15 October 2026, attendees posted their own clips, and Elon Musk said Tesla needed an audience "to prove that it's not AI".

You don't need a stunt budget. Answer two questions first, then film only the moments that prove both.

> [!FORM] Your demo shot list
> 1. What is the most extreme thing your product can do?
> 2. How do you make people believe it happened?
> 3. Which three moments would you film to prove both? One line each.

For software, show the real input, the real output and anything you fixed by hand (like the spreadsheet-to-app example in *Give strangers a reason to care*). Better yet, let people try it themselves. Hacker News's [Show HN rules](https://news.ycombinator.com/showhn.html) ask for something people can play with, ideally without a sign-up.

## Learn video as a medium

Kunal Gandhi argues you should master video as a medium, whatever platform it lands on, because the skill is holding your audience's imagination on screen. In his experience, product previews, demo videos and quirky humour beat everything else, and skipping video is a common launch mistake. He points to thirdweb's launch videos as a model.

Meta's research backs the product part. In a [study of over 600 video ads](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative), viewers remembered ads best when they showed the product and kept the message short. Meta notes that people watch feeds fast and without sound, so a feed video has to make sense on mute.

> [!TRICK] Cut the demo for the feed
> 1. Open on the extreme moment, not on a logo or a founder intro.
> 2. Add captions or on-screen text so it works on mute.
> 3. Cut anything that doesn't prove the feat.

Start with a plain screen recording of the extreme moment. When you want it to look premium, see *Make product videos feel premium*.

> [!CHECK] What two questions should a launch demo answer?

> [!CHECK] Viewers say your software launch demo looks staged. What do you show instead?
> The real input, the real output, and anything you fixed by hand. A launch demo's job is to make people think "this is real", and the hand fixes make it believable.

> [!CHECK] Why did Tesla jump the Roadster over a whole row of cars, live in front of a crowd?
> Jumping the whole fleet is extreme enough to send to friends, and doing it live made it impossible to dismiss as CGI or AI.
