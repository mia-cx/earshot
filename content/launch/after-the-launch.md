> [!TLDR]
> - Judge a launch by the people it brings in, not the views. Count trials, relevant DMs and sales calls.
> - Before launch, assign who answers product questions and who follows up on leads.
> - Answer in the thread. Send a DM only with a clear reason.
> - Log what people did and asked, not their compliments, then run a second wave within 1 to 2 weeks.

## Count people, not views

Eric Ries calls numbers like views and hits [vanity metrics](https://tim.blog/2009/05/19/vanity-metrics-vs-actionable-metrics/). They feel good, yet they don't tell you what to do next. Actionable metrics track real people, counted per customer or per group.

Paul Graham [makes the same point about launches](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html). All you need from one is an initial core of users, and you keep them with personal attention a bigger company can't match.

Measure your launch by the **trials, relevant DMs and sales calls** it produces. Views tell you a post travelled, not that it worked.

If a prospect's source is unclear, ask where they found you. Track only what you can verify.

## Split the replies before you post

Launch day spreads replies across your posts, other people's quotes and creators' comment sections. **Assign owners before you post** so everyone knows who answers product questions and who follows up on business leads.

Answer where people asked, right in the thread. Send a DM only when you have a clear reason tied to what they said, and check their profile first. A like on its own tells you nothing.

## Keep one doc of what people ask

Launch replies tell you what to fix, explain and post next. Capture them in one shared doc so the lessons outlast launch week.

Not everything people say is real evidence. Rob Fitzpatrick's [*The Mom Test*](https://www.momtestbook.com/) says to [ask about specifics in the past](https://learningleader.com/robfitzpatrick/), not opinions about the future. "Love this!" is a compliment. "I tried it on last month's invoices and it missed two" is data. Log the second kind.

> [!TRICK] Turn the launch thread into assets
> 1. Log recurring questions, objections, product issues and unexpected use cases, with links and the answer you gave.
> 2. Turn repeated questions into your FAQ and confusing features into walkthroughs. Route feedback to someone who can act on it.
> 3. When you ship a fix someone asked for, go back and tell them.
> 4. Ask permission to feature people's tests and results, credit them, and use them as your next posts.

## Run a second wave

Your first wave shows which creators sparked useful conversations and which use case buyers keep raising. Put both to work.

- Rebook creators who brought useful conversations within 1 to 2 weeks. Give them something new: a deeper walkthrough, a customer use case, or an update that answers first-round questions.
- Give the use case buyers keep raising more space in your next demo and on the landing page.

> [!TRICK] Pre-launch checklist
> - A clear claim with evidence.
> - Different angles for different sharers.
> - Creators who understand the product and its audience.
> - A team ready to answer and follow up.
> - An easy way to try the product or get in touch.

The tactics here come from one launch agency founder's practice (0xfJuan, Ark Media), not measured data, including the 1 to 2 week timing.

> [!DO] Set up the launch log
> Create a shared doc with five columns: question or comment, link, type (question, objection, issue, use case), answer given, owner. Add the first row from any reply you've had this month.

> [!CHECK] What should you measure a product launch by, instead of views?
> The trials, relevant DMs and sales calls it produced. Views are a vanity metric that only shows the post travelled.

> [!CHECK] Someone liked your launch post but didn't reply. Should you DM them?
> No. A like on its own tells you nothing, so DM only when you have a clear reason that fits what someone said, and check their profile first.

> [!CHECK] Why log every launch question, objection and surprise use in one shared doc?
> It tells you what to fix, explain and post next, and it outlasts launch week. Repeated questions become your FAQ, and confusing features get a walkthrough.
