> [!TLDR]
> - Start marketing in week one alongside the product, and begin with positioning so every later message lands.
> - Days 1 and 2: write down what you do and for whom, then turn it into positioning. No posting yet.
> - Days 3 and 4: post with what you have (screenshots, Loom demos) and anchor on the ecosystem you build in.
> - Days 5 to 7: show proof, ship something people can click, and recap the week in public.
> - After week one, build community in two phases: onboard and listen, then activate and reward.

## Why marketing starts in week one

Almost every failed startup had a product, Gabriel Weinberg and Justin Mares argue in [*Traction*](https://leananalyticsbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Traction-1-3.pdf). What it lacked was enough customers. Their 50% rule splits your time evenly between product and traction, in parallel, because traction work shows which message and niche land.

Positioning comes first. April Dunford's [positioning exercise](https://hackernoon.com/obviously-awesome-a-product-positioning-exercise-604e8ced841e) explains why. The market you claim sets the context people judge you by, and your messaging follows from that choice.

## Days 1 and 2: get clear before you post

Kunal Gandhi wrote this plan for a marketer joining a day-0 crypto project, yet nothing in it depends on crypto. Use it for any product with no marketing yet, including your own.

**Day 1, clarity.** Before you open X or design a banner, answer five questions in one doc: what you do, what problem you solve, which category you compete in, why you're better, and who exactly you're for. If you can't answer them, you don't understand the product yet.

> [!FORM] Your clarity doc
> 1. What do we do? What problem do we solve? Give one short, precise answer.
> 2. Which market category do we compete in?
> 3. Why is our approach the best? What sets us apart?
> 4. Target audience: who exactly are we for?

**Day 2, positioning.** Run the Goal → Audience → Creative → Channel plan from *Fit your strategy on one page*. Map three messaging pillars: "What we build", "Why now" and "Who it's for". Check them with the founders and developers, because marketing is not separate from the product.

## Days 3 and 4: post with what you have

**Day 3, first three posts.** Use screenshots, mockups or Loom demos, and don't wait for a designer. Show what you're building, make your promise clear, and build anticipation.

> [!TRICK] The early-testers post
> "We're building X for Y. Early testers wanted."

**Day 4, ecosystem.** Anchor your launch on the platform or ecosystem you build in, and use your integrations as the onboarding story. Run a thunderclap where founders, friends and ecosystem people post together (see *Line up your launch-day voices*).

## Days 5 to 7: proof, something to touch, a recap

**Day 5, social proof.** Highlight every integration, quote replies from users, and show the feedback loop. Talk with people, not at them, and make early users feel seen.

**Day 6, ship something touchable.** Put out a waitlist, a leaderboard or a beta quest, anything people can click or join. Execution beats polish.

**Day 7, recap.** Write a weekly thread covering what worked, what didn't and what's next. Tag the people who helped. You build momentum in public.

## After week one

Build community in two phases: **onboard and listen**, then **activate and reward**. Use chat groups (Discord, Telegram) for depth and X for reach. Show the product at hackathons and partner showcases.

Keep the weekly recap thread going, track daily work in one place, and review your numbers weekly to double down on what works (*Fit your strategy on one page* covers the weekly review).

This plan is Gandhi's own playbook. The post got 223 bookmarks, yet it reports no outcomes, so treat it as a sensible order of work rather than a proven result.
