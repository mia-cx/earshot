> [!TLDR]
> - Plan content as two steps: the story you're best placed to tell, then an angle on it few others take.
> - A content map is a tree: 3 to 4 pillars, 5 to 7 topics each, 5 to 10 ideas per topic, 4 to 5 angles per idea.
> - Build it from your brand documents plus research on what has performed in the last 6 to 12 months.
> - Collect patterns like an artist collects references. Never copy a post 1:1.

## A content map is a tree

Most people sit down to post facing a blank page. With a content map, **you pick the next angle off a tree** you already built.

The shape Dan Koe uses:

| Level | How many | What it is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pillars | 3 to 4 | Broad themes you'll own |
| Topics | 5 to 7 per pillar | Areas inside each theme |
| Ideas | 5 to 10 per topic | Specific points likely to perform |
| Angles | 4 to 5 per idea | Ways into the same idea |

Even at the low end that's 3 × 5 × 5 × 4 = 300 angles. Koe says one run gives about six months of ideas. That's unverified, but six months at 3 to 5 posts a week is 78 to 130 posts, so the arithmetic leaves room to spare.

## Merge your lens with what already works

Joe Pulizzi's Content Inc. model [starts with two steps](https://www.magnificent.com/magnificent-stuff/content-inc-model). First is the sweet spot, "the story you should be telling", where he advises you to "be the leading expert in a very small particular niche." Second is the content tilt: "telling a different story that can break through all the content clutter".

Koe's map combines both through two inputs. Your brand and voice documents (from *Write down what makes you you*) give the pillars and your lens: how you see things differently. Research gives the proof: which ideas and formats have performed in those topics over the past 6 to 12 months.

Either input alone fails. Pure lens gives original posts nobody asked for. Pure research gives the same posts as everyone else.

> [!TRICK] Generate the map with one prompt
> Attach your brand document and voice document, then run this. Eden is Koe's own research tool; swap in whatever research tool or web-enabled assistant you have.
>
> ```
> I want you to create a research backed content map: 3-4 unique and broad content pillars that branch into 5-7 topics that are both based on ideas that are working, through my own unique lens based on [@ brand document] and [@ content document]. Branching off each topic should be 5-10 ideas that are likely to perform well, with 4-5 angles branching off of each of those ideas.
>
> Look over my brand and content docs, pull potential pillars and my lens out of it, then use Eden to research what's performing across platforms in those topics. We want a unique content map, but we want to merge it with patterns that have been proven to perform over the past 6-12 months.
>
> Put the output in an easy to read document.
> ```

## Collect references, don't copy

Researching what performs can feel like selling out. Koe frames it as an artist's inspiration board: you pull patterns (a structure, a hook shape, a format), then fill them with your own ideas.

That risk is real. When everyone studies the same top posts, everyone starts sounding the same. Jenni Romaniuk of the Ehrenberg-Bass Institute [sees this in brands](https://contagious.cz/images/IO/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets.pdf): creatives draw from the same environment and end up alike. [Anil Doshi and Oliver Hauser](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) found the same with AI: stories written from AI-generated ideas scored better yet came out more alike. Your lens is what keeps research from flattening you.

One way to stay original: research platforms you don't post on. Koe says that's where you find the ideas nobody on your platform has used yet. *Reuse formats that already work* goes deeper on copying the format and never the content.

> [!FORM] Your content map
> 1. Pillars and perspective: What could you talk about for hours (everything)? Which 2-3 matter most? How do you come at each differently ("through psychology", "as a former nurse")? What do people ask your advice on? What do you keep explaining?
> 2. Pillar 1: a broad theme you'll own.
> 3. Pillar 2: a broad theme you'll own.
> 4. Pillar 3: a broad theme you'll own.
> 5. Under the pillar you know best, list 5 topics: areas inside that theme.

> [!DO] Sketch the top of your map
> Fill in your content map above: 3 pillars, then 5 topics under the one you know best. Download it, and run the full prompt later, once your brand document exists.

> [!CHECK] What are the four levels of a content map, from broadest to narrowest?
> Pillars, topics, ideas and angles. A typical map has 3 to 4 pillars, 5 to 7 topics each, 5 to 10 ideas per topic and 4 to 5 angles per idea.

> [!CHECK] Research shows a post structure that has performed well in your topic. How do you use it without copying?
> Take the pattern, such as the structure, hook shape or format, and fill it with your own ideas. Never copy a post 1:1.

> [!CHECK] Why does a content map need both your own lens and research on what performs?
> Your lens alone gives original posts nobody asked for. Research alone gives the same posts as everyone else. Pulizzi calls the two halves the sweet spot and the content tilt.
