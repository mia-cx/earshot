# Launching: literature notes

Research behind `content/launch/`. Each lesson lists the principles it rests on and an independent audit of every factual claim in it. Verdicts: **supported** (the source says this), **overstated** (the source says something narrower), **misattributed** (true, but the cited source doesn't say it), **disputed**, **unsupported**, or **one person's claim** (a practitioner's number, labelled as such in the lesson). Where a claim wasn't supported, the lesson was corrected and the row says how. All sources were opened and read on 2026-11-23. ftc.gov and Reddit Help block scripts, so those were read through the Wayback Machine's 2026 copies, and the two journal abstracts through Crossref.

## Sources used across the module

| Source | What it says (paraphrased) |

| [Paul Graham, "Do Things That Don't Scale" (2013)](https://paulgraham.com/ds.html) | The most common unscalable thing founders must do is recruit users manually; you go out and get them. Start with a deliberately narrow market ("keeping a fire contained at first to get it really hot"). Make early users delighted (Wufoo's handwritten notes). The "Big Launch" usually doesn't work: all you need from a launch is an initial core of users. |
| [Gabriel Weinberg & Justin Mares, *Traction* (2014), sample chapters 1 to 3](https://leananalyticsbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2014/08/Traction-1-3.pdf) | Bullseye: brainstorm every channel, rank them in three rings, prioritise about three, run cheap tests ("four Facebook ads vs. forty", "a few hundred dollars"), then focus on the one that works. "At any stage in a startup's lifecycle, one traction channel dominates." The 50% rule: spend half your time on product and half on traction, in parallel. Founders' channel biases are the main failure. |
| [Jonah Berger & Katherine Milkman, "What Makes Online Content Viral?", *Journal of Marketing Research* (2012)](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1509/jmr.10.0353) | Abstract read. Across three months of New York Times articles, content that evokes high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) is shared more; low-arousal sadness less. Surprising, interesting and practically useful content is also shared more. Experiments show the arousal effect is causal. |
| [Meta Foresight (Facebook IQ), "Capturing Attention in Feed" (2016)](https://www.facebook.com/business/news/insights/capturing-attention-feed-video-creative) | Fors Marsh tests: 0.25 seconds of exposure is enough for people to recall mobile feed content at a significant rate. | [Robert Cialdini / Influence at Work: the principles of persuasion](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) | Social proof: when uncertain, people look to what others, especially similar others, do. | [Simone Wies, Alexander Bleier & Alexander Edeling, "Finding Goldilocks Influencers", *Journal of Marketing* (2023)](https://www.ama.org/2022/10/11/finding-goldilocks-influencers-why-high-follower-count-may-not-be-the-best-driver-of-engagement-on-social-media/) | Read via the AMA's summary of the paper ([journal page](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00222429221125131)). 1,700+ influencers across 800+ Instagram campaigns: engagement with sponsored posts follows an inverted U by follower count. | [Rob Fitzpatrick on *The Mom Test*, The Learning Leader show notes](https://learningleader.com/robfitzpatrick/) | The three rules: talk about their life instead of your idea; ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future; talk less and listen more. ## Show the product at its extreme (`the-demo.md`)

**Principles.** Awe and surprise are among the strongest drivers of sharing (Berger & Milkman). In feeds, video ads that show the product and get the message across succinctly are best recalled, and feed video must work without sound (Meta 2016). The most credible demo is one people can try (Show HN guidelines).

## Make your landing page look designed on purpose (`landing-page.md`)

**Principles.** First impressions of a page's look form within milliseconds, and low visual complexity with a familiar (prototypical) layout reads as most appealing (Tuch et al.). Designing several options in parallel beats refining one at a time (Dow et al.). Interface motion should be short, and the more often it plays the shorter it should be (Laubheimer).

Sources, read 2026-11-24: [Tuch, Presslaber, Stöcklin, Opwis & Bargas-Avila, *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies* (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijhcs.2012.06.003), via the [Google Research abstract page](https://research.google/pubs/the-role-of-visual-complexity-and-prototypicality-regarding-first-impression-of-websites-working-towards-understanding-aesthetic-judgments/) (ScienceDirect blocks scripts); [Dow, Glassco, Kass, Schwarz, Schwartz & Klemmer, *ACM TOCHI* 17(4) (2010)](https://hci.stanford.edu/publications/2010/parallel-prototyping/ParallelPrototyping2010.pdf), abstract on page 1; [Laubheimer, NN/g (2020)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/animation-duration/). Lindgaard et al.'s 50 ms study (2006) was not opened (Taylor & Francis returned 403), so the lesson relies on Tuch et al. alone.

| Claim | Verdict | Source that settles it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 119 website screenshots; effects of complexity and prototypicality on appeal within 17 ms | **supported** (study 1: 119 screenshots at 50 to 1,000 ms; study 2: 17 to 50 ms) | Tuch et al. |
| Low complexity and a familiar layout are judged most appealing | **supported** ("websites with low VC and high PT were perceived as highly appealing") | Tuch et al. |
| "Stand out" should not mean an unfamiliar layout | **supported** in principle; reconciles Leo's "stand out" with Tuch et al. | Tuch et al. |
| Most developer landing pages look the same; agents return the average template | **one person's claim**, labelled (Leo) | lessons.sh #02 |
| Pick audience, style and palette first; lock them in the prompt | **one person's claim**, labelled (Leo) | lessons.sh #02 |
| Novices designing web ads in parallel got more clicks, better expert ratings and more varied designs than serial | **supported** | Dow et al., abstract |
| Five structurally different hero layouts, then combine | **one person's claim** (Leo); parallel exploration is **supported** | Dow et al.; lessons.sh #02 |
| Build the core 70 to 80%, then one signature touch that fits | **one person's claim**, labelled (Leo) | lessons.sh #02 |
| The four product/signature pairings | **one person's claim**, labelled as Leo's examples | lessons.sh #02 |
| Most interface animations: 100 to 500 ms; at 500 ms "animations start to feel like a real drag" | **supported** (quotes match) | Laubheimer |
| Respect reduce-motion settings | **supported** | Laubheimer |
| One hero animation plus short hover states; no slow headline fade-ins | **one person's claim**, labelled (Leo) | lessons.sh #02 |

## Work the conversation after launch (`after-the-launch.md`)

**Principles.** Views are vanity metrics; trials and conversations per person are actionable (Ries). Early users need delight and personal attention (Graham). Feedback is only useful when it is about what people did, not compliments (Fitzpatrick).
