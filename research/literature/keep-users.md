# Keep and convert: literature notes

Research behind `content/keep-users/`. Every source below was opened and read; where only an abstract or summary was readable, the note says so. Claims are marked **confirmed**, **disputed** or **unverified**.

## retention.md: Keep the users you get

### Principles and sources

**Retention pays, but it doesn't grow you on its own.**

- Fred Reichheld (Bain), [*Prescription for cutting costs*](https://media.bain.com/Images/BB_Prescription_cutting_costs.pdf) (2001), full PDF read. Customers generate more profit each year they stay: they buy more, cost less to serve, refer others and pay a premium. "In financial services, for example, a 5% increase in customer retention produces more than a 25% increase in profit." He also says not every customer can be profitable and long-standing, so segment and invest only in the ones that can.
- Amy Gallo, HBR, [*The Value of Keeping the Right Customers*](https://hbr.org/2014/10/the-value-of-keeping-the-right-customers) (2014), opening only (paywall). Repeats "5 to 25 times" more to win a new customer than keep one, and hedges it herself: depends on the study and the industry. Cites Reichheld for 5% retention giving 25% to 95% more profit.
- Riebe, Wright, Stern and Sharp, [*How to grow a brand: Retain or acquire customers?*](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbrese/v67y2014i5p990-997.html), *Journal of Business Research* 67(5), 2014. Abstract read. Using doctors' prescribing panels and a residential-finance tracker, "for both growth and decline, unusual acquisition plays a much stronger role than unusual defection." Acquisition also explained far more variation in profit in their simulation.
- Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, [*Effective brand growth: Acquisition or retention?*](http://marketingscience.info/effective-brand-growth-acquisition-or-retention) Read in full. Summarises Double Jeopardy (small brands have fewer buyers who are slightly less loyal), says acquisition is "roughly twice as important" as reduced defection for growing brands, and that much defection is outside a marketer's control. Concludes retention still matters (don't alienate customers) but doesn't drive share growth.

**Define activation as a repeated key action, not a single first step.**

- Casey Winters (ex-Pinterest, Grubhub), [*Why Onboarding is the Most Crucial Part of Your Growth Strategy*](https://www.caseyaccidental.com/p/why-onboarding-is-the-most-crucial-part-of-your-growth-strategy) (2017). Read. Two questions: how often should a user get value (frequency target, benchmarked on the offline equivalent), and which action shows they got it (key action). Activation = "people who are still doing [key action] at [set interval] at [this period after signup]". Pinterest: weekly savers four weeks after signup. Grubhub used a leading indicator: a second order within 30 days.
- Lenny Rachitsky with Casey Winters, [*What is good retention*](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/what-is-good-retention-issue-29) (2020). Read. Practitioner benchmarks from about 20 growth leaders, not a study. Six-month user retention, good / great: consumer social ~25% / ~45%, consumer transactional ~30% / ~50%, consumer SaaS ~40% / ~70%, SMB and mid-market SaaS ~60% / ~80%, enterprise SaaS ~70% / ~90%.
- Reforge's setup / aha / habit moments (Winters and Shaun Clowes): the guide page wouldn't render; not verified, not cited.

**Behaviour-triggered messages work because they arrive at the moment of action; holdouts prove it.**

- BJ Fogg, [Fogg Behavior Model](https://behaviormodel.org/) and [Prompts](https://behaviormodel.org/prompts/). Read. B=MAP: behaviour happens when motivation, ability and a prompt converge at the same moment. Without a prompt the behaviour doesn't happen. Prompt a simple first step; don't pack too much into one prompt.
- Ron Kohavi, Diane Tang and Ya Xu, [*Trustworthy Online Controlled Experiments*](https://experimentguide.com/) (Cambridge University Press, 2020). Book page read only; cited for the method (randomised control group), no figures taken.
- Kohavi and Thomke, HBR, [*The Surprising Power of Online Experiments*](https://hbr.org/2017/09/the-surprising-power-of-online-experiments) (2017), opening read. A Bing headline change sat unprioritised for six months; a cheap A/B test showed a 12% revenue lift. Point: intuition misjudges ideas, the control group doesn't.

**Check-ins: a stated next step works as a default.**

- Johnson and Goldstein, *Do Defaults Save Lives?* (*Science*, 2003). Publisher and repository pages blocked (403). Only a Kellogg student blog summary was readable. **Not verified**; kept out of the lesson.

### Not used

- Nir Eyal, *Hooked*: no primary page opened. Fogg's model is the primary source its trigger idea builds on, so the lesson cites Fogg.

## paid-ads.md: Run paid ads you can measure

### Principles and sources

**Platforms report correlation; only an experiment shows what ads cause.**

- Gordon, Zettelmeyer, Bhargava and Chapsky, [*A Comparison of Approaches to Advertising Measurement: Evidence from Big Field Experiments at Facebook*](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/gordon_b/files/fb_comparison.pdf), *Marketing Science* 38(2), 2019. Abstract and introduction read. Fifteen RCTs, 500 million user-experiment observations. Observational methods "generally" overestimated ad effects (sometimes underestimated); "in half of our studies, the estimated percentage increase in purchase outcomes is off by a factor of three across all methods."
- Blake, Nosko and Tadelis, [*Consumer Heterogeneity and Paid Search Effectiveness*](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w20171/w20171.pdf) (NBER 2014; *Econometrica* 2015). Abstract and introduction read. eBay field experiments: returns from paid search are "a fraction of conventional non-experimental estimates"; brand-keyword ads had no measurable short-term benefit; non-brand ads helped new and infrequent users but average returns were negative.
- Lewis and Rao, [*The Unfavorable Economics of Measuring the Returns to Advertising*](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/qjecon/v130y2015i4p1941-1973.html), *QJE* 130(4), 2015. Abstract read. 25 large field experiments: median ROI confidence interval over 100 percentage points wide; informative experiments can need more than 10 million person-weeks.
- Vaver and Koehler (Google), [*Measuring Ad Effectiveness Using Geo Experiments*](https://research.google/pubs/measuring-ad-effectiveness-using-geo-experiments/) (2011). Abstract read. Randomly assign non-overlapping regions to treatment or control; control regions show what would have happened.
- [Google Meridian](https://developers.google.com/meridian) and [Meta Robyn](https://facebookexperimental.github.io/Robyn/): both open-source marketing mix models; both calibrate with experiment results (Meridian GeoX; Robyn takes geo and lift studies).

**Clean conversion data.**

- Meta for Developers, [Deduplicate Pixel and server events](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/deduplicate-pixel-and-server-events/). Read. Browser `eventID` must match server `event_id`, and event names must match; duplicates within 48 hours are discarded.
- Meta for Developers, [Conversions API best practices](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/conversions-api/best-practices/). Read. Event Match Quality is scored out of 10; only matched events feed attribution and optimisation. No numeric "good" threshold given.

**Match the page to the ad.**

- Raluca Budiu, NN/g, [*Information Scent*](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/information-scent/) (2020). Read. Users click on a guess of a page's value; landing pages that don't deliver on the link's promise get abandoned, and broken promises use up trust.
- Evan Miller, [*How Not To Run an A/B Test*](https://www.evanmiller.org/how-not-to-run-an-ab-test.html) (2010). Read. Peeking and stopping when significant inflates false positives (26.1% in his simulation of checking after every observation). Fix the sample size in advance.

**Creative.**

- Nielsen, [*When it comes to advertising effectiveness, what is key?*](https://www.nielsen.com/insights/2017/when-it-comes-to-advertising-effectiveness-what-is-key/) (2017). Read. Across ~500 FMCG campaigns, creative remained the largest driver of sales lift; media's share rose to 36%. Supports "creative matters most"; says nothing about creative volume.

**Fake testimonials.**

- FTC, [*Final rule banning fake reviews and testimonials*](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2024/08/federal-trade-commission-announces-final-rule-banning-fake-reviews-testimonials) (14 August 2024). Read. Bans reviews and testimonials that misrepresent who wrote them, explicitly including AI-generated fake reviews.

## pricing-signals.md: Read your pricing from your numbers

### Principles and sources

**Some customers cost more than they pay; time-based costing finds them.**

- Robert S. Kaplan, HBS Working Knowledge, [*A Balanced Scorecard Approach to Measure Customer Profitability*](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/a-balanced-scorecard-approach-to-measure-customer-profitability) (2005). Read. In nearly every study, 15 to 20% of customers produce 100% or more of profits; in his insurance example the bottom 5% lost the equivalent of 30% of profits. Remedy: time-driven activity-based costing, which needs only the cost per hour of each resource and the time each activity takes. Fix order: improve your own process, price menu options to cover their cost, cross-sell, and drop the account only as a last resort.
- Kaplan and Narayanan, *Measuring and Managing Customer Profitability* (2001): full text not reachable; **not verified**, not cited.

**Pricing is under-managed.**

- Patrick Campbell (ProfitWell), [*A Guide to Unlocking Growth with Optimized Pricing*](https://www.mindtheproduct.com/a-guide-to-unlocking-growth-with-optimized-pricing/) (2020). Read. "ProfitWell research found that companies only spend 10 hours or less per year on pricing." No method or sample given, and it comes from a vendor. Used only as a labelled practitioner claim.
- Marn and Rosiello, HBR, [*Managing Price, Gaining Profit*](https://hbr.org/1992/09/managing-price-gaining-profit) (1992), opening read: "The right price can boost profit faster than increasing volume will." The often-quoted 1%-price-to-11%-profit figure was behind the paywall; **not verified**, kept out.
