# Literature: Content that builds a brand

Research notes behind `content/content/`. Every source below was opened and read for this pass, unless it sits under "Not verified". Claim checks mark each number or factual claim in the lessons as **confirmed**, **disputed** or **unverified**.

## Sources read

| Source | What it says (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- |
| [Les Binet and Peter Field: The Long and the Short of It, IPA presentation (2013)](https://ipa.co.uk/media/5811/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final.pdf) | Meta-analysis of 996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories in the IPA Databank. Rational, short-term messages give a big direct effect that decays quickly; emotional brand building gives a smaller effect that decays slowly and builds over years. Fame campaigns quadruple efficiency. About 60:40 brand to activation is the best balance. Short-term metrics alone can mislead. |
| [Alison Hoad, IPA blog: The next chapter for The Long and The Short of It (2023)](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it) | The 2013 report warned that over-use of short-term metrics damages long-term profitability. The 60:40 split held in later updates (62:38 in 2018). |
| [Joe Pulizzi on the Content Inc. model, Magnificent Marketing interview](https://www.magnificent.com/magnificent-stuff/content-inc-model) | Six steps: find the sweet spot, the content tilt ("a different story that can break through all the content clutter"), build the base ("one content type, and one platform, and consistently deliver on time"), harvest an audience, diversify, monetise. His own experience: six months of silence, first signs in months six to nine, no direct revenue until after twelve months. |
| [Kirsti Lang, Buffer: Consistent posting study (2025)](https://buffer.com/resources/consistent-posting-study/) | 100,000+ Buffer users over 26 weeks. Those who posted in 20+ weeks got about 4.5 to 5 times the median engagement per post of those who posted in 4 weeks or fewer; gains flattened after about 21 weeks. Observational, from a scheduling-tool company. |
| [Jenni Romaniuk interview, Contagious (2022)](https://contagious.cz/images/IO/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets.pdf) | Distinctiveness lets people attach what they see to your brand without effort. Consistency of form matters while you build an asset. Creatives who draw from the same environment drift into sameness. |
| [Joanna Wiebe, Copyhackers: How to find your message using review mining (2014)](https://copyhackers.com/2014/10/amazon-review-mining/) | "Instead of writing your message, steal it": copy the prospect's own words from reviews. For a rehab centre, a headline lifted from an Amazon review ("If you think you need rehab, you do") got over 400% more clicks than the control in her test. |
| [Benjamin Franklin: Autobiography (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/148) | Franklin learned to write by taking notes on Spectator essays, rewriting them days later from the notes, comparing with the original and fixing his faults; he also turned essays into verse and back, and shuffled his notes to practise arranging ideas. Read in the Gutenberg text mirrored on archive.org. |
| [Anil Doshi and Oliver Hauser: Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content (Science Advances, 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) | Stories written with AI-generated ideas were rated better but were more similar to each other. Read via the abstract and the [ScienceDaily release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/07/240712222127.htm). |
| [Stephanie Stahl, Content Marketing Institute: B2B Content Marketing Benchmarks, Budgets, and Trends, Outlook for 2025 (2024)](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/b2b-research/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research-2025) | 980 B2B respondents. Most effective content types: videos 58%, case studies and customer stories 53%, e-books and white papers 45%. A year earlier, case studies ranked first. These are marketers' own ratings, not measured sales. |
| [Google Search Central: Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) | Scaled content abuse includes rewording content with synonyms or automated transformations, and running many sites to hide scaled content. Link spam includes links in guest posts and articles distributed on other sites, and low-value content made to manipulate links. |

### Not verified (kept out of the lessons)

- Austin Kleon, *Steal Like an Artist* (2012): the original essay has been replaced by a book page, and the archive was unreachable.
- Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test*: the book's site doesn't state the rules.

## Build a brand, not a viral hit

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One viral post followed by silence means no brand | **Confirmed** in principle: short-term spikes decay; brand effects build over years | Binet and Field 2013 |
| A brand's ideas play out over 6 to 12 months of posts | Unverified, Koe's plan | @thedankoe |
| Pick one platform | **Confirmed** as Pulizzi's "build the base" step | Pulizzi |
| Post 3 to 5 times a week | Unverified, one person's opinion | @notkvtwts |
| Expect a few months before it pays off | **Disputed**: Pulizzi saw nothing for six months and no revenue for twelve; Binet and Field measure brand effects in years. Lesson corrected. | Pulizzi; Binet and Field |
| A founder's own account drives more early growth than a company page or ads | **Unverified**: one team's experience. Lesson and check card now say so. | @kunalvg |
| Consistency beats volume | **Confirmed** as a correlation: weekly posters got about 4.5 times the engagement per post; gains flattened after 21 weeks | Buffer 2025 |

## Write down what makes you you

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Use the exact words your reader uses | **Confirmed** as practice, with one reported test (400%+ more clicks) | Wiebe 2014 |
| Don't clone a voice; note what makes it distinctive | **Confirmed** in principle: a cue shared with others is weaker | Romaniuk 2022 |
| Study writers you admire to build your own style | **Confirmed** as a classic method, now given as steps | Franklin |
| Koe's process works for clients | Unverified, anecdotal; Koe founded the tool the article recommends | @thedankoe |

## Plan six months of ideas in one sitting

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 3 × 5 × 5 × 4 = 300 angles | **Confirmed** (arithmetic) | |
| One run gives about six months of ideas | Unverified, but 3 to 5 posts a week for 26 weeks is 78 to 130 posts, well under 300 | @thedankoe |
| Merge your own lens with what performs | **Confirmed** in principle: Pulizzi's sweet spot plus content tilt | Pulizzi |
| Pure research gives the same posts as everyone else | **Confirmed** in principle: shared inputs make work more alike | Doshi and Hauser 2024; Romaniuk 2022 |
| Research platforms you don't post on to find unused ideas | Unverified | @thedankoe |

## Turn one conversation into ten posts

| Claim | Verdict | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Customer conversations give content in their words | **Confirmed** in principle | Wiebe 2014 |
| Case studies move B2B buyers more than anything else | **Disputed**: in CMI's 2025 survey, B2B marketers rated video first (58%) and case studies second (53%); case studies ranked first the year before and first among tech marketers. Lesson corrected. | CMI 2024 |
| Kunal's team wrote 50+ case studies and it sped up sales | Unverified, no numbers given | @kunalvg |

| Spread to other platforms | Pulizzi puts "diversify" after you've built one base; the lesson now says so | Pulizzi |
