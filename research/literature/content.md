# Literature: Content that builds a brand

Research notes behind `content/content/`. Every source below was opened and read in November 2026, unless it sits under "Not verified". Each number or factual claim in the lessons carries an audit verdict:

- **Supported**: the source says this.
- **Overstated**: the source says something narrower, older, or with caveats the lesson dropped.
- **Misattributed**: true or close to it, but the cited source doesn't say it.
- **Disputed**: stronger evidence says otherwise.
- **Unsupported**: no source, and nothing else confirms it.
- **One person's claim**: a practitioner's number or experience, labelled as such in the lesson.

Where a claim wasn't supported, the row names the fix made in the lesson.

## Sources read

| Source | What it says (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- |
| [Les Binet and Peter Field: The Long and the Short of It, IPA presentation (2013)](https://ipa.co.uk/media/5811/long_and_short_of_it_presentation_final.pdf) | Meta-analysis of 996 campaigns, 700 brands, 83 categories in the IPA Databank. Rational messaging gives a "big direct effect, but decays quickly"; emotional priming gives a "smaller effect on sales" that "decays slowly" and builds over years. "No long term effects without short term", "long term effects drive growth and profit", "You need both". Fame campaigns quadruple efficiency. About 60:40 brand to activation. These are advertising campaigns, not organic posts. |
| [Alison Hoad, IPA blog: The next chapter for The Long and The Short of It (2023)](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it) | The 2013 report warned that over-use of short-term metrics damages long-term profitability. The 60:40 split held in later updates (62:38 in 2018). |
| [Joe Pulizzi on the Content Inc. model, Magnificent Marketing interview](https://www.magnificent.com/magnificent-stuff/content-inc-model) | Six steps. The sweet spot is "the story you should be telling"; the content tilt is "telling a different story that can break through all the content clutter". Building the base: "Instead of telling your story on every channel you can possibly think of, you actually focus on one content type, and one platform, and consistently deliver on time." Diversifying comes fifth. "Be the leading expert in a very small particular niche." Pulizzi's own blog: "radio silence" for six months, first signs in months six to nine, no direct revenue until after twelve. |
| [Kirsti Lang, Buffer: Consistent posting study (29 January 2025)](https://buffer.com/resources/consistent-posting-study/) | 100,000+ Buffer users over 26 weeks, grouped by how many weeks they posted. Those who posted in 20+ weeks got "450% more engagement per post" (more than 5 times, by median) than those who posted in 4 weeks or fewer; engagement peaked at 21 weeks, then "started to plateau and eventually decline". Observational, from a scheduling-tool company. |
| [Jenni Romaniuk interview, Contagious (2022)](https://contagious.cz/images/IO/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets.pdf) | An asset's strength is fame and uniqueness: "The more you share it with others the weaker it is." Consistency of form matters while you build an asset. "Creatives source from their environment and a lot of them are drawing from the same environment", which brings sameness. |
| [Joanna Wiebe, Copyhackers: How to find your message using review mining (2014)](https://copyhackers.com/2014/10/amazon-review-mining/) | "Instead of writing your message, steal it." For the Beachway rehab centre, a headline lifted from an Amazon review ("If you think you need rehab, you do") brought ">400% more clicks" on the button than the control, "Your Addiction Ends Here", and >20% more form submits. |
| [Benjamin Franklin: Autobiography (Project Gutenberg)](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/148) | Franklin made "short hints of the sentiment in each sentence" of Spectator papers, laid them by "a few days", rewrote them, compared with the original and corrected his faults; he turned tales into verse and back, and "jumbled my collections of hints into confusion" to learn "method in the arrangement of thoughts". Gutenberg blocks automated requests; read from the Gutenberg mirror at aleph.gutenberg.org. |
| [Anil Doshi and Oliver Hauser: Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content (Science Advances, 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) | Writers given AI story ideas produced stories rated more creative, better written and more enjoyable, "especially among less creative writers", but "more similar to each other than stories by humans alone". Read via the abstract on Crossref and the [ScienceDaily release](https://www.sciencedaily.com/releases/2024/07/240712222127.htm); science.org blocks automated requests. |
| [Stephanie Stahl, Content Marketing Institute: B2B Content Marketing Benchmarks, Budgets, and Trends, Outlook for 2025 (2024)](https://contentmarketinginstitute.com/b2b-research/b2b-content-marketing-trends-research-2025) | 980 B2B respondents. Most effective content types: videos 58%, case studies and customer stories 53%, e-books and white papers 45%. A year earlier, case studies ranked first. Marketers' own ratings, not measured sales. The 2026 edition (1,015 respondents) doesn't repeat the content-type ranking. |
| [Google Search Central: Spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) | Scaled content abuse includes automated transformations like synonymizing, and creating multiple sites to hide scaled content. Link spam includes optimised links "in articles, guest posts, or press releases distributed on other sites". |
