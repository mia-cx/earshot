# Literature: Writing that gets read

Research notes behind `content/writing/`. Every source below was opened and read on 2026-11-23, unless it sits under "Not verified".

Each lesson ends with a claim check. Verdicts: **supported** (the source says this), **overstated** (the source says something narrower), **misattributed** (true, but not what the cited source says), **disputed** (stronger evidence says otherwise), **unsupported** (no source confirms it), **one person's claim** (a practitioner's view, labelled as such in the lesson). Where a claim needed a fix, the last column says what the lesson says now.

## Sources read

| Source | What it says (paraphrased) |
| --- | --- |
| [Stanford Magazine: Getting Attention (2007)](https://stanfordmag.org/contents/getting-attention) | Profile of Chip Heath and *Made to Stick*. The curse of knowledge. Elizabeth Newton's tapper study: tappers predicted listeners would name 50% of songs; listeners named 3 of 120. Sticky ideas share simplicity, unexpectedness, concreteness, credibility, emotion and story. |
| [Hoa Loranger, NN/g: Plain Language Is for Everyone, Even Experts (2017)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/plain-language-experts/) | Usability study with experts in science, tech and medicine. They also wanted short, scannable text. Suggested reading level: 6th to 8th grade for general audiences, 10th to 12th for specialists; beyond 12th is too much effort even for highly educated readers. "No one has ever complained that a text was too easy to understand." |
| [Jakob Nielsen, NN/g: How Users Read on the Web (1997)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-users-read-on-the-web/) | 79% of test users scanned new pages; 16% read word by word. Concise text tested 58% more usable, scannable 47%, objective (no hype) 27%, all three together 124%. Users detested promotional "marketese". |
| [Kara Pernice, NN/g: F-Shaped Pattern of Reading on the Web (2017)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/f-shaped-pattern-reading-web-content/) | The F-pattern is the default when text has no strong cues. Seen on desktop and mobile. Counter it: most important points in the first two paragraphs, headings and lines that lead with the informative words, bullets, bold key phrases. |
| [Kate Moran, NN/g: Reading Content on Mobile Devices (2016)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-content/) | 276 participants. No practical difference in comprehension between phone and computer, but readers slowed down for difficult text on a phone. Still recommends brevity for mobile. |
| [Therese Fessenden, NN/g: Scrolling and Attention (2018)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolling-and-attention/) | Eyetracking on web pages: 57% of viewing time above the fold, 74% in the first two screenfuls. |
| [George Orwell: Politics and the English Language (1946)](https://www.orwellfoundation.com/the-orwell-foundation/orwell/essays-and-other-works/politics-and-the-english-language/) | Six rules, including: never a long word where a short one will do; if a word can be cut, cut it; never the passive where you can use the active. |
| [Twitter: SimClusters README in the-algorithm (2023)](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm/blob/main/src/scala/com/twitter/simclusters_v2/README.md) | About 145,000 communities found from the follow graph of the top 20M producers. A tweet's embedding adds the interest vector of each user who favourites it, so a post's community placement comes from who engages with it. |
| [xAI: X For You feed algorithm (GitHub)](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm) | X's 2026 feed code. Candidate sources are Thunder (posts from accounts you follow), Phoenix retrieval, SimClusters ("an additional source of posts from accounts the viewer does not follow") and a popular-posts list. Phoenix ranks the candidates. Read 2026-11-23. |
| [Christensen Institute: Jobs to Be Done](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/) | A job is "the progress they're trying to make" in a particular circumstance. People "pull them into their lives to make progress". |
| [George Loewenstein: The Psychology of Curiosity (Psychological Bulletin, 1994)](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-2909.116.1.75) | Curiosity is a form of deprivation caused by noticing a gap in one's knowledge. Read via the abstract only. |
| [Marianne Aubin Le Quéré and J. Nathan Matias: When curiosity gaps backfire (Scientific Reports, 2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-024-81575-9) | Registered report. Meta-analysis of 8,977 headline A/B tests from the Upworthy Research Archive (Upworthy ran over 27,000 tests). When the baseline headline is too vague, more concreteness gets more clicks; when it is too concrete, more concreteness gets fewer. The model predicts more concreteness would help only 8.7% of Upworthy's headlines and hurt 50.9%. Of nine earlier lab and field studies of curiosity-style headlines, three found a negative effect, two positive, four none. Facebook changed its feed to reduce curiosity headlines. |
| [John Caples: Tested Advertising Methods (1932; 5th edition revised by Fred E. Hahn, 1997)](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3350190W) | Read in the 1997 5th edition [text on the Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/tested-advertising-methods). Among headlines that worked in keyed tests, self-interest did best, news next, curiosity third. "Curiosity by itself is seldom enough", but curiosity combined with news or self-interest helps. "Write a number of headlines for every advertisement and then select the best one." Whether the 1932 first edition words it the same way was not checked. Open Library refused connections during this pass; a Wayback copy of the page confirms the link. |
| [BJ Fogg: Fogg Behavior Model](https://behaviormodel.org/), [Prompts](https://behaviormodel.org/prompts/), [Ability](https://behaviormodel.org/ability/) | Motivation, ability and a prompt must converge at the same moment. "Without a Prompt, the target behavior will not happen." "Call to action" is another name for a prompt. Scaling back the behaviour raises ability. |
| [Influence at Work (Robert Cialdini): Principles of Persuasion](https://www.influenceatwork.com/7-principles-of-persuasion/) | Consistency: people act in line with what they've already said or done. Homeowners who first agreed to a small Drive Safely window card were four times as likely to accept a large lawn sign. |
| [Jenni Romaniuk interview, Contagious (2022)](https://contagious.cz/images/IO/jenni-romaniuk-on-distinctive-assets.pdf) | Distinctiveness is "looking like you and not like others". Change weakens distinctive assets; if you play with an asset while building it, "you're creating different ones". The more you share an asset with other brands, the weaker it is. Assets should never be a "fashion item". |
| [Kate Moran, NN/g: The Four Dimensions of Tone of Voice (2016)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-of-voice-dimensions/) | Tone varies on four scales: funny to serious, formal to casual, respectful to irreverent, enthusiastic to matter-of-fact. |
| [Kate Moran, NN/g: The Impact of Tone of Voice on Users' Brand Perception (2016)](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/tone-voice-users/) | Paired pages that differed only in tone. "Casual, conversational, and moderately enthusiastic tones performed best." A humorous insurance page was friendlier but less trustworthy. "Humor is extremely risky." |
| [Maurice Jakesch, Jeffrey Hancock and Mor Naaman: Human heuristics for AI-generated language are flawed (PNAS, 2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07271) | Six experiments, 4,600 participants: people could not detect AI-written self-presentations in professional, hospitality and dating contexts. They relied on flawed cues (first-person pronouns, contractions, family topics). Read via the arXiv abstract. |
| [Elizabeth Clark et al.: All That's 'Human' Is Not Gold (ACL, 2021)](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.565/) | Untrained evaluators told GPT-3 text from human text at chance level, across stories, news articles and recipes. Read via the abstract. |
| [Anil Doshi and Oliver Hauser: Generative AI enhances individual creativity but reduces the collective diversity of novel content (Science Advances, 2024)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn5290) | 293 writers, 600 evaluators. Writers could ask GPT-4 for a three-sentence story idea, then wrote the story themselves. AI-idea stories were rated more creative and better written, mostly for less creative writers, but were more similar to each other. Read in full on [PubMed Central](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11244532/); the journal page blocked automated access. |

### Not verified (kept out of the lessons)

- Eugene Schwartz, *Breakthrough Advertising* (1966): the five levels of awareness. Only secondary summaries were reachable.
- Joseph Sugarman, *The Adweek Copywriting Handbook* (2007): "the sole purpose of the first sentence ... is to get you to read the second sentence". Found only in third-party notes.
- Freedman and Fraser (1966), the foot-in-the-door paper itself: the PDF is a scan with no text layer. The finding is used through Cialdini's own summary.
- Molina, Sundar et al. on clickbait: the paper page refused access.
- Ann Handley, *Everybody Writes*: not opened.
