# Foundations: literature notes

Module `content/foundations/`. Every source below was opened and read on 2026-11-23. Paraphrases, not quotes, unless marked.

Each lesson ends with a claim check. Verdicts: **supported** (the source says this), **overstated** (the source says something narrower), **misattributed** (true, but not what the cited source says), **disputed** (stronger evidence says otherwise), **unsupported** (no source confirms it), **one person's claim** (a practitioner's view, labelled as such in the lesson). Where a claim needed a fix, the last column says what the lesson says now.

## 1. Attention is the job

### Principles and sources

- **Brands grow mainly by winning more buyers, which takes mental and physical availability.** Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, [How do you measure How Brands Grow?](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/how-do-you-measure-how-brands-grow). Summarises Byron Sharp, *How Brands Grow* (2010): brands compete with every brand in the category and grow mainly through penetration (more buyers). Mental availability: buyers easily think of the brand when buying and recognise it quickly. Physical availability: buyers can buy it in a variant, quantity and price that fits.
- **Growth and decline come mostly from acquisition, not retention.** Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, [Answering critics](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/answering-critics) (Byron Sharp). Cites Riebe, Wright, Stern & Sharp, "How to grow a brand: Retain or acquire customers?", *Journal of Business Research* 67(5), 2014, 990–997 ([DOI 10.1016/j.jbusres.2013.08.005](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbusres.2013.08.005), metadata checked on Crossref; full text paywalled, not read): sales growth and decline were largely due to over- or under-performance in acquisition. Also says bigger brands have slightly higher loyalty on every metric (Double Jeopardy), so "brands grow only by acquisition" is a misreading.
- **Long-term brand building and short-term activation both pay; the best mix leaned to brand.** IPA, [The next chapter for The Long and the Short of It](https://ipa.co.uk/knowledge/ipa-blog/the-next-chapter-for-the-long-and-the-short-of-it) (Alison Hoad, 2023). Les Binet and Peter Field's 2013 IPA report found the optimum budget ratio was 60:40 brand building to activation; their 2018 *Effectiveness in Context* found 62:38. As short-term campaigns rose, effectiveness (very large business effects in IPA cases) fell.
- **Marketing starts at the product.** Seth Godin, [The circles of marketing](https://seths.blog/2012/07/the-circles-of-marketing/) (2012). Outer circle: hype and promotion. Next in: a story that resonates with a tribe. Next in: what surrounds the product (usability, support, atmosphere). Centre: the product itself. "When in doubt, when your marketing isn't working, the answer is easy: go one circle in." (quote). The post's diagram labels the rings: product; usability and support; story, price, community and tribe; ads, spin, buzz, hype, clicks and "getting the word out".
- **Creator economy forecast.** Goldman Sachs Research, [The creator economy could approach half-a-trillion dollars by 2027](https://www.goldmansachs.com/insights/articles/the-creator-economy-could-approach-half-a-trillion-dollars-by-2027) (19 April 2023, Eric Sheridan): total addressable market could roughly double to $480B by 2027 from $250B "today".
- **Creators vs traditional media in ad revenue.** The Guardian, [Social media creators to overtake traditional media in ad revenue this year](https://www.theguardian.com/media/2025/jun/10/social-media-creators-to-overtake-traditional-media-in-ad-revenue-this-year) (10 June 2025): reports WPP Media's forecast that content on YouTube, TikTok and Instagram will attract more ad income in 2025 than traditional media content.

## 2. Say what you are, for whom, against what

### Principles and sources

- **Positioning is context: how your product is a leader at delivering something a well-defined set of customers cares a lot about.** April Dunford, [A Quickstart Guide to Positioning](https://www.aprildunford.com/post/a-quickstart-guide-to-positioning) (15 March 2021), summary of *Obviously Awesome* (2019). Five components: competitive alternatives, differentiated capabilities, value, target customers, market category. Each depends on the others. Start with competitive alternatives ("what would customers do if our solution didn't exist?"), then what you have that they lack, then the value, then who cares most, then the category. Credits Christensen's jobs to be done for the starting point. Traps: listing "phantom competitors" nobody actually compares you with; forgetting "do nothing", which is the status quo you must position against (Dunford puts enterprise software's loss to "no decision" at about 25% of deals, an estimate).
- **The positioning statement is the wrong tool; positioning is a choice among several.** April Dunford, [Obviously Awesome: a product positioning exercise](https://hackernoon.com/obviously-awesome-a-product-positioning-exercise-604e8ced841e) (7 November 2016). Any product can be positioned several ways; a fill-in-the-blanks statement hides that. Earlier five-step version: let go of where you came from, isolate uniqueness, value, who cares, market frame of reference.
- **Positioning lives in the prospect's mind.** Al Ries, [The History of Positioning](https://ries.com/about/history-of-positioning). Ries and Jack Trout's three-part series "The Positioning Era Cometh" ran in *Advertising Age* in April and May 1972. The 1981 book *Positioning: The Battle for Your Mind* was not opened.
- **Few buyers see brands as different.** Jenni Romaniuk, Byron Sharp and Andrew Ehrenberg, ["Evidence concerning the importance of perceived brand differentiation"](https://byronsharp.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2008/04/different1.pdf), *Australasian Marketing Journal* 15(2), 2007. Across 17 markets in Australia and the UK, an average 11% of a brand's own customers called it "different", 10% "unique", 17% either. Conclusion: perceived differentiation is not necessary to be bought; put distinctiveness at the centre of brand strategy.
- **Differentiation vs distinctiveness.** Byron Sharp, [Differentiation vs distinctiveness](https://byronsharp.wordpress.com/2008/04/10/differentiation-vs-distinctiveness/) (2008). Differentiation is a reason to buy; distinctiveness is the brand looking like itself.
- **Jobs to be done: people hire products to make progress in a circumstance.** Clayton Christensen Institute, [Jobs to Be Done theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/). A job is the progress people try to make toward a goal in particular circumstances, with functional, social and emotional sides. The theory looks past demographics and product attributes to explain why people choose.

## 3. Research a market in an afternoon

### Principles and sources

- **AI-generated research is for hypotheses, not decisions.** Maria Rosala and Kate Moran, NN/g, [Synthetic Users: If, When, and How to Use AI-Generated "Research"](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/synthetic-users/) (21 June 2024). Synthetic users give shallow or overly favourable feedback. Useful for desk research and generating hypotheses; real research needs real users.
- **Ask about specific past behaviour, not opinions about your idea.** Rob Fitzpatrick, *The Mom Test* (2013), [book site](https://www.momtestbook.com/). The book itself was not opened, and its site does not list the rules. The three rules are confirmed through the book's passage as quoted on [Goodreads](https://www.goodreads.com/quotes/10333501-talk-about-their-life-instead-of-your-idea-ask-about): talk about their life instead of your idea; ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future; talk less and listen more.
- **What buyers say and what buyers did differ.** Christensen Institute, [Jobs to Be Done theory](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), Bob Moesta's condominium case. Prospects said they didn't need a formal dining room. In interviews with actual buyers the dining room table kept coming up: people moved once they knew what to do with it. Larger dining rooms, moving services and two years of storage followed.
- **Reviews hold the buyer's own words.** Joanna Wiebe, Copyhackers, [Amazon review mining for copywriting](https://copyhackers.com/2014/10/amazon-review-mining/) (2014). For a rehab centre, Wiebe read 500+ Amazon reviews of six books on addiction. A headline lifted from one review ("If you think you need rehab, you do") got more than 400% more clicks than the control and more than 20% more form submits on the next page (Wiebe's own test, one client).
- **Ad libraries must show when each ad ran.** EU Digital Services Act, [Article 39](https://www.eu-digital-services-act.com/Digital_Services_Act_Article_39.html) (Regulation 2022/2065; text read on an unofficial mirror). Very large platforms must keep a public ad repository for as long as an ad runs and one year after, including the ad's content, who paid, and the period it ran. Spend and results are not on the required list.
- **What Meta's Ad Library shows.** Meta for Developers, [Archived Ad reference](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/marketing-api/reference/archived-ad/) (the Ad Library API). Every ad has its text (`ad_creative_bodies`) and `ad_delivery_start_time`, "when an advertiser wants to start delivering an ad". Spend and impressions are "available only for POLITICAL_AND_ISSUE_ADS". Meta's help pages render only with JavaScript and could not be read.

## 4. Fit your strategy on one page

### Principles and sources

- **Strategy is a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent actions. Goals are not strategy.** Richard Rumelt, [The perils of bad strategy](https://www.mckinsey.com/capabilities/strategy-and-corporate-finance/our-insights/the-perils-of-bad-strategy), *McKinsey Quarterly* (June 2011), adapted from *Good Strategy Bad Strategy* (2011). Four hallmarks of bad strategy: failure to face the challenge, mistaking goals for strategy, bad strategic objectives, fluff. The kernel: a diagnosis, a guiding policy and coherent actions. Without a named obstacle you have "a stretch goal or a budget or a list of things you wish would happen". Read through the Wayback Machine because mckinsey.com blocked the fetch.
- **Focus on controllable inputs to move outputs.** Jeff Bezos, [2009 letter to shareholders](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1018724/000119312510082914/dex991.htm). Amazon focuses on "the controllable inputs to our business", which it believes is the most effective way to maximise financial outputs over time. Of 452 goals for 2010, none mention net income, gross profit or operating profit.
- **The GACC brief.** Emily Kramer, MKT1, [The GACC Marketing Brief](https://newsletter.mkt1.co/p/the-gacc-marketing-brief-the-best) (27 July 2021). Goals, audience, creative (unique take), channels (distribution); Kramer evolved it while leading marketing at Asana. Everything a team makes should ladder up to a goal or a perception. The rest of the post is paywalled; it does not give a reason for the order in the free part.
- **Bounce rate in GA4.** Google, [Engagement rate and bounce rate](https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/12195621?hl=en). Bounce rate is the share of sessions that were not engaged: 10 seconds or less, no key event, and fewer than 2 page or screen views.

## 5. Map the whole funnel

### Principles and sources

- **Pirate metrics.** Dave McClure, [Startup Metrics for Pirates: AARRR!](https://www.slideshare.net/dmc500hats/startup-metrics-for-pirates-long-version), from his Ignite Seattle talk in August 2007 ([500 Hats post, 6 September 2007](https://web.archive.org/web/2010/http://500hats.typepad.com/500blogs/2007/09/startup-metrics.html)). Acquisition, Activation ("users enjoy 1st visit: 'happy' user experience"), Retention, Referral, Revenue.
- **A flat retention curve is the best sign of product-market fit.** Brian Balfour, [Building a Growth Framework Towards a $100 Million Product](https://brianbalfour.com/essays/hubspot-growth-framework-100m). "The best indication for Market Product Fit are retention curves that flatten." Balfour was VP of Growth at HubSpot ([his about page](https://brianbalfour.com/about)).
- **Growth comes mostly from acquisition.** Ehrenberg-Bass Institute, [Answering critics](https://marketingscience.info/news-and-insights/answering-critics), citing Riebe et al. 2014 (see lesson 1). Loyalty rises a little as brands grow (Double Jeopardy), so the two move together.
